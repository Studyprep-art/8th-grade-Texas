## Passage 1 — The First Catch (literary nonfiction)

(1) I was nine the summer my grandfather taught me to fish. Every morning we rowed to the middle of the
gray lake and waited. And waited. For three days I caught nothing but boredom.

(2) "Fishing isn't about catching," Grandpa said on the fourth morning, reeling in another empty line.
"It's about paying attention."

(3) I didn't understand him. I wanted a fish—something to hold up and photograph. But I kept casting,
because he kept casting.

(4) On the fifth day, my line went tight. My heart leaped. I pulled too hard, and the fish slipped
free. I groaned, but Grandpa only smiled. "Now you're paying attention," he said.

(5) The next cast, I waited. I felt the smallest tug, counted to three, and lifted gently. A silver
fish broke the surface, flashing in the sun. I had done it.

(6) Years later, I still remember what the lake taught me that summer. Patience, it turns out, is its
own kind of catch.

**1.** In paragraph 1, "nothing but boredom" suggests the narrator felt — &nbsp; A. excited  B. impatient and bored  C. afraid  D. proud

**2.** The narrator keeps casting mainly because — &nbsp; A. he already loves fishing  B. his grandfather keeps casting  C. he is forced to  D. he wants to row the boat

**3.** When Grandpa says "Now you're paying attention," he means the narrator — &nbsp; A. caught a fish  B. is finally focused and learning  C. should go home  D. is tired

**4.** The central lesson of the memoir is that — &nbsp; A. fishing is boring  B. patience is valuable  C. grandfathers are wise  D. lakes are gray

**5.** The author wrote this mainly to — &nbsp; A. inform about fish species  B. share a meaningful personal experience and its lesson  C. sell fishing gear  D. argue a point

**6.** Which line **best** shows that the narrator learned patience? &nbsp; A. "I wanted a fish"  B. "I pulled too hard, and the fish slipped free"  C. "The next cast, I waited... and lifted gently"  D. "we rowed to the middle"

**7.** "Patience... is its own kind of catch" is an example of — &nbsp; A. simile  B. metaphor  C. alliteration  D. onomatopoeia

**SCR 1.** *(2 points)* What does the narrator learn by the end of the memoir? Support your answer with **evidence from the text.**

## Passage 2 — Save Our Library (persuasive)

(1) Our school library is more than a room full of books. It is the heart of our school—and it needs
our help.

(2) Every day, students visit the library to read, study, and find a quiet place to think. For some, it
is the only place they can borrow books they could never afford to buy. Closing it, or cutting its
hours, would leave these students behind.

(3) Imagine a school where no one reads for joy, where research means guessing instead of learning. Is
that the school we want?

(4) We can act now. Sign the petition, attend the board meeting, and tell the district that our library
matters. Together, our voices are loud enough to save it.

(5) Don't wait until the shelves are empty. Stand up for your library today.

**8.** The author's purpose is to — &nbsp; A. inform about library hours  B. persuade readers to support the library  C. tell a story  D. describe a book

**9.** The author's main message is that — &nbsp; A. libraries are outdated  B. the community should act to save the library  C. books are expensive  D. students should study more

**10.** "Is that the school we want?" is a — &nbsp; A. statistic  B. rhetorical question meant to persuade  C. definition  D. stage direction

**11.** Which detail **best** supports the idea that the library helps students who cannot buy books? &nbsp; A. "a room full of books"  B. "the only place they can borrow books they could never afford to buy"  C. "attend the board meeting"  D. "the heart of our school"

**12.** "Stand up for your library today" is an example of — &nbsp; A. a call to action  B. a counter-argument  C. a citation  D. a simile

**13.** The author most likely wrote this because the library is — &nbsp; A. brand new  B. at risk of closing or cuts  C. too big  D. never used

**14.** Which is the **best** summary? &nbsp; A. The library has books.  B. The author urges readers to take action to save a valued school library that some students depend on.  C. Board meetings are long.  D. Reading is fun.

**SCR 2.** *(2 points)* Why does the author believe the library is important? Use **evidence from the passage.**

## Paired Set — The Old Oak & Why Trees Matter

**Text A — "The Old Oak" (poem)**
> For a hundred years it stood, / arms open to the sky, / shade for the weary traveler, / home where
> the songbirds fly. / It asked for nothing, gave for free— / the patient, giving tree.

**Text B — "Why Trees Matter"**
> Trees do quiet, important work. They clean the air by taking in carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen.
> Their roots hold soil in place and soak up rainwater, preventing floods. Their shade cools
> neighborhoods and lowers energy costs. Protecting old trees protects us, too.

**15.** In Text A, calling the tree "patient" and "giving" is an example of — &nbsp; A. personification  B. simile  C. irony  D. hyperbole

**16.** Text A is written in — &nbsp; A. paragraphs  B. lines and a stanza with rhyme  C. a script  D. a chart

**17.** The main idea of Text B is that trees — &nbsp; A. are pretty  B. provide important environmental benefits  C. live a long time  D. give birds homes

**18.** Both texts present trees as — &nbsp; A. useless  B. valuable and worth protecting  C. dangerous  D. temporary

**19.** Which idea appears in Text B but **not** in Text A? &nbsp; A. Trees give shade  B. Trees clean the air and prevent floods  C. Trees are homes for birds  D. Trees are old

**20.** Text A mainly aims to — &nbsp; A. inform with data  B. express appreciation for a tree  C. argue for a law  D. give directions
