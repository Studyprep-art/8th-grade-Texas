# Reading Strategy Lessons

These lessons teach the reading skills STAAR tests again and again. Each is tagged with its TEKS and
marked **★ Readiness** or **○ Supporting**.

---

## Part A · Vocabulary  (2A ○, 2B ★, 2C ○)

- **2B · Context clues ★** — When a word is unfamiliar, read the words *around* it. Definitions,
  examples, synonyms, antonyms, and the overall sense of the sentence or paragraph point to the meaning.
- **2A · Reference tools ○** — A dictionary or glossary gives a word's meaning, syllables,
  pronunciation, origin, and part of speech. (A dictionary is available on the real test.)
- **2C · Greek and Latin roots ○** — Word parts unlock meaning: **ast** (star → *astronomy*), **qui**
  (quiet/rest → *tranquil*), **path** (feeling → *sympathy*), **mand/mend** (order → *command*),
  **duc** (lead → *conduct*).

> **Worked example.** "The *arid* desert had not seen rain in years." *Arid* must mean very dry —
> the context (desert, no rain) shows it.

> **Try It.** In "His *gregarious* nature made him the center of every party," what does *gregarious*
> likely mean? *(Outgoing/sociable — context of parties and being central.)*

---

## Part B · Comprehension  (5C ○, 5E ★, 5F ★, 5G ★, 5H ★)

- **5F · Inference + evidence ★** — An inference is a smart conclusion based on **clues in the text**
  plus what you already know. Always find the words that support it.
- **5G · Key ideas ★** — Decide what matters most; separate the main point from small details.
- **5H · Synthesize ★** — Combine ideas (within a text or across two texts) to build a new understanding.
- **5E · Make connections ★** — Link the text to other texts, to the world, and to experience.
- **5C · Predictions ○** — Use genre and text features to predict, then confirm or correct as you read.

> **Worked example (inference).** If a character "slammed the door and refused to speak," you can
> **infer** she is angry — the evidence is her actions, even though the text never says "angry."

> **Try It.** A passage says the sky darkened, wind rose, and people hurried indoors. What can you
> infer? *(A storm is coming — supported by the weather clues.)*

---

## Part C · Response to Reading  (6C ★, 6D ★)

- **6C · Text evidence ★** — Support every answer by pointing to a specific word, phrase, or sentence
  in the passage. This is the heart of STAAR reading.
- **6D · Paraphrase and summarize ★** — Restate a text in your own words (paraphrase) or give a short
  version of just the main points in order (summarize), keeping the meaning accurate.

> **Strategy:** For "Which sentence **best** supports…?" questions, decide your answer first, then hunt
> for the line that proves it. The right choice is the one that *directly* backs the idea — not one
> that is merely true.

---

## Part D · Literary Elements  (7A ○, 7B ★, 7C ★, 7D ○)

- **7B · Character motivation ★** — Why characters act as they do, and how that drives events and the
  resolution of the conflict.
- **7C · Non-linear plot ★** — Out-of-order storytelling: **flashback** (a jump to the past),
  **foreshadowing** (a hint of what's coming), **subplots**, and parallel plots — compared with
  straight, linear order.
- **7A · Theme ○** — The message or lesson, developed through characters and events.
- **7D · Setting ○** — When and where the story happens, and how it shapes characters' values and beliefs.

> **Try It.** A story opens at a funeral, then jumps back to show the character as a child. What plot
> device is used? *(Flashback.)*

---

## Part E · Author's Purpose and Craft  (9A ★, 9B–9G ○)

- **9A · Purpose and message ★** — Why the author wrote the text: to **inform, persuade, or
  entertain** — and the message within it.
- **9B · Text structure ○** — How organization (cause–effect, compare–contrast, sequence,
  problem–solution) serves the purpose.
- **9C · Print and graphic features ○** — Headings, captions, bold words, charts — and why the author
  uses them.
- **9D · Figurative language ○** — Similes, metaphors (including **extended metaphor**), personification.
- **9E · Literary devices ○** — **Irony** and **multiple points of view**.
- **9F · Mood, voice, and tone ○** — The feeling of a text (mood), the author's personality (voice),
  and attitude toward the subject (tone).
- **9G · Rhetorical devices and logical fallacies ○** — **Analogy** and **juxtaposition**; faulty
  reasoning such as **bandwagon appeals** and **circular reasoning**.

> **Worked example (tone).** Word choices like "cramped, gloomy, and endless" create a **negative,
> heavy tone** — the author's attitude shows through the words.

> **Try It.** An ad says "Everyone is switching to BrightSmile — don't be left behind!" Which fallacy
> is this? *(Bandwagon appeal.)*

---

### Reading Skills Checklist
- ☐ 2A–C Vocabulary: context clues, reference tools, Greek/Latin roots
- ☐ 5C–H Comprehension: predict, connect, infer with evidence, find key ideas, synthesize
- ☐ 6C–D Response: use text evidence; paraphrase and summarize
- ☐ 7A–D Literary elements: theme, character motivation, non-linear plot, setting
- ☐ 9A–G Author's craft: purpose, structure, features, figurative language, devices, tone, rhetoric
