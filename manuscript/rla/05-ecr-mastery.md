# Extended Constructed Response (ECR) Mastery

The Writing section includes **1 Extended Constructed Response** — a full multi-paragraph essay worth
**10 points**, tied to a reading passage. It is the single biggest-point task on the RLA test, so it
deserves real practice. *(Composition standards 11B informational, 11C argumentative, 11D
correspondence; and 6B comparing sources.)*

---

## What the Essay Must Have

The 10-point rubric rewards an essay with:
- A clear **central idea or claim** (a thesis).
- **Coherent organization** — introduction, body paragraphs, conclusion.
- **Sufficient development** — specific **evidence** and explanation, not just general statements.
- **Effective use of language and conventions** — varied sentences, precise words, correct grammar.

> **Key Idea:** Points come from a clear thesis, organized paragraphs, and **specific evidence**
> explained in your own words. A blank or one-paragraph answer cannot score high — always write a full,
> organized essay.

---

## Two Common Prompt Types

- **Informational (11B ★)** — Explain or inform about a topic, using a clear controlling idea.
- **Argumentative (11C ★)** — Take a position on an issue and defend it with reasons and evidence;
  acknowledge the other side.
- *(Correspondence, 11D ○, may appear in revising/editing as a letter or email.)*

---

## A 4-Step Essay Plan

1. **Plan (2–3 min).** Decide your thesis. Jot 2–3 supporting points and the evidence for each.
2. **Introduction.** Hook + restate the topic + state your **thesis/claim.**
3. **Body paragraphs (2–3).** One point each: state it → give **evidence** (from the passage) →
   **explain** it. Use transitions (*first, in addition, however, for example*).
4. **Conclusion.** Restate the thesis in fresh words and leave a final thought.

> **Argumentative tip:** Add a sentence that names the **counter-argument** and answers it ("Some may
> say…, but…"). This strengthens your position.

---

## Annotated Sample (Argumentative, shortened)

**Prompt:** *Should students have longer recess? Write an argumentative essay.*

> **Many schools are cutting recess, but students actually learn better with more of it.** *(clear
> claim)* First, recess gives the brain a break. The article states that "students who move during the
> day focus longer in class." *(evidence)* This shows that play is not wasted time — it helps learning.
> *(explanation)* In addition, recess builds friendships and reduces stress. Some people argue recess
> takes away learning minutes, **but** research shows rested students finish work faster. *(counter-
> argument answered)* For these reasons, schools should give students longer recess. *(conclusion)*

**Why this would score well:** clear claim, organized points, real evidence with explanation, a
counter-argument, and a conclusion.

---

## Scoring at a Glance

| Score band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **High (8–10)** | Clear thesis, well-organized, specific evidence explained, strong conventions |
| **Middle (4–7)** | A thesis and some organization, but thin evidence or several errors |
| **Low (1–3)** | Unclear or missing thesis, little development, many errors |
| **0** | Blank, off-topic, or too brief to score |

> **Strategy:** Spend a couple of minutes planning before you write — an organized essay almost always
> scores higher than a longer, messy one. Always use **evidence from the passage**, and leave a minute
> to check spelling and punctuation.
