## Passage 1 — The Audition

(1) Maya's fingers hovered over the piano keys. The recital hall was empty except for Ms. Alvarez, who
waited with a clipboard and a patient smile. For weeks Maya had practiced the same piece until her
hands ached, yet now every note seemed to hide from her.

(2) "Whenever you're ready," Ms. Alvarez said.

(3) Maya's heart thudded. She thought of the school talent show two years earlier, when she had frozen
on stage and walked off to a silent crowd. The memory still stung. She almost reached for her bag,
ready to leave.

(4) Then she remembered her grandmother's words: "Courage isn't the absence of fear, Maya. It's
playing anyway." Maya took a slow breath, closed her eyes, and began.

(5) The first notes trembled, but by the second measure her hands remembered what her mind had
forgotten. The music filled the empty hall, rising and falling like waves. When the final chord faded,
Maya opened her eyes. She was smiling.

(6) "That," said Ms. Alvarez, setting down her clipboard, "was worth the wait."

(7) Maya walked out lighter than she had entered. The fear had not vanished—but she had played anyway,
and that had made all the difference.

**1.** In paragraph 1, the word *hovered* most nearly means — &nbsp; A. rested lightly  B. pressed hard  C. slipped off  D. disappeared

**2.** Why does Maya almost reach for her bag? &nbsp; A. She forgot her music  B. She recalls freezing at the talent show  C. She dislikes Ms. Alvarez  D. She is bored

**3.** The grandmother's words suggest that courage means — &nbsp; A. never feeling afraid  B. avoiding challenges  C. acting in spite of fear  D. practicing more

**4.** Which statement **best** expresses a theme of the passage? &nbsp; A. Talent is something you are born with  B. Facing your fear leads to growth  C. Music is too hard for most people  D. Teachers are strict

**5.** The mood at the end of the passage is — &nbsp; A. tense and fearful  B. calm and hopeful  C. angry  D. confused

**6.** Which sentence **best** shows that Maya has overcome her fear? &nbsp; A. "Her hands ached."  B. "Every note seemed to hide from her."  C. "She had played anyway, and that had made all the difference."  D. "The recital hall was empty."

**7.** Paragraph 3, about the talent show two years earlier, is an example of — &nbsp; A. foreshadowing  B. flashback  C. climax  D. setting

**SCR 1.** *(2 points)* How does Maya change from the beginning to the end of the passage? Support your answer with **evidence from the text.**

## Passage 2 — Cities Buzz Again

(1) Not long ago, honeybees were a rare sight in big cities. Today, hives sit on rooftops, in community
gardens, and even atop hotels. Urban beekeeping is growing fast—and cities are better for it.

(2) Bees are pollinators. As they gather nectar, they move pollen from flower to flower, helping plants
produce fruits, vegetables, and seeds. A single hive can pollinate millions of flowers in a day. In a
city, that means healthier parks, gardens, and street trees.

(3) Urban hives can even be healthier than rural ones. City gardens bloom with many kinds of flowers,
so bees enjoy a varied diet. And because many city gardeners avoid heavy pesticides, urban bees may
face fewer harmful chemicals than bees near large farms.

(4) Beekeeping also connects people. Neighbors who tend a shared hive learn about nature and work
together. Schools use rooftop hives to teach science. Local honey, sold at markets, supports small
businesses.

(5) Of course, beekeeping takes training. Beekeepers must know how to handle bees safely and keep hives
healthy. But with care, a city hive brings sweet rewards—for people, plants, and the bees themselves.

**8.** The central idea of the passage is that — &nbsp; A. bees are dangerous in cities  B. urban beekeeping benefits cities  C. honey is too expensive  D. farms no longer have bees

**9.** The author's main purpose is to — &nbsp; A. entertain with a story  B. inform readers about the benefits of urban beekeeping  C. sell honey  D. warn people to stay away from bees

**10.** Paragraph 3 is organized mainly by — &nbsp; A. comparing urban and rural hives  B. telling a story in order  C. listing dangers  D. defining a single word

**11.** As used in paragraph 2, *pollinators* are organisms that — &nbsp; A. make honey only  B. move pollen between flowers  C. build hives  D. sting people

**12.** Which detail **best** supports the idea that city bees may be healthier than rural bees? &nbsp; A. "hives sit on rooftops"  B. "City gardens bloom with many kinds of flowers, so bees enjoy a varied diet."  C. "Beekeeping takes training."  D. "Local honey supports small businesses."

**13.** From paragraph 3, the reader can infer that heavy pesticide use — &nbsp; A. helps bees  B. has no effect on bees  C. can be harmful to bees  D. makes more honey

**14.** Which is the **best** summary of the passage? &nbsp; A. Bees sting, so cities ban them.  B. Urban beekeeping is growing and benefits plants, people, and bees, though it takes training.  C. Honey tastes sweet.  D. Schools have rooftops.

**SCR 2.** *(2 points)* Explain **one** way urban beekeeping helps a city. Use **evidence from the passage.**

## Paired Set — Rooftop Garden & Green Roofs

**Text A — "Rooftop Garden" (poem)**
> Above the traffic's restless hum, / a quiet green begins to climb— / tomatoes red as stoplights, /
> basil bright with summer time. / Here the city tilts its face / toward sun and rain and seed, / and
> proves that even concrete / can hold what gardens need.

**Text B — "Green Roofs"**
> Rooftop gardens, or "green roofs," do more than grow food. They soak up rainwater, cool buildings in
> summer, and give insects and birds a place to rest. Some cities now pay building owners who plant them.

**15.** In Text A, "tomatoes red as stoplights" is an example of — &nbsp; A. metaphor  B. simile  C. personification  D. alliteration

**18.** Both texts suggest that rooftop gardens — &nbsp; A. are impossible in cities  B. bring nature into the city  C. should replace all parks  D. are only for growing food

**19.** Which idea appears in Text B but **not** in Text A? &nbsp; A. Plants grow on rooftops  B. Green roofs soak up rainwater and cool buildings  C. Gardens need sun  D. Cities are busy
