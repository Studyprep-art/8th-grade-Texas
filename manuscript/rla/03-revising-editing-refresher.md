# Revising and Editing Refresher

In the Writing section you fix and improve short passages. **Revising** makes writing clearer and
better organized; **editing** corrects grammar and conventions. *(Standards 10B, 10C, 10D.)*

---

## Revising — Making Writing Better  (10B ★, 10C ★)

- **10B · Structure and development ★** — A strong piece has an **introduction, clear transitions,
  coherent paragraphs, and a conclusion** (10B·i), and develops its ideas with **specific facts,
  details, and examples** (10B·ii).
- **10C · Revising for quality ★** — Improve **clarity, development, organization, style, word choice,
  and sentence variety.** Combine choppy sentences; cut repetition; choose precise words.

> **Worked example.** "The dog was big. The dog was loud. The dog ran." → Revise for sentence variety:
> "The big, loud dog ran across the yard."

---

## Editing — Correcting Conventions  (10D)

**10D · Standard English conventions** includes:
- **10D·i · Complete sentences ★** — Use complete, correct sentences with **subject-verb agreement**;
  avoid **run-ons, comma splices, and fragments.**
- **10D·ii · Verb tense and voice ★** — Keep verb tenses **consistent and appropriate**; use active
  and passive voice correctly.
- **10D·iii · Prepositions ○** — Use prepositions and prepositional phrases correctly; don't let them
  confuse subject-verb agreement.
- **10D·iv · Pronoun-antecedent agreement ○** — A pronoun must match its noun in number and gender.
- **10D·v · Capitalization ○** — Capitalize correctly.
- **10D·vi · Punctuation ○** — Commas in nonrestrictive phrases/clauses, **semicolons, colons,
  parentheses.**
- **10D·vii · Spelling ★** — Spell correctly, including commonly confused words: **its/it's,
  affect/effect, there/their/they're, to/two/too.**

> **Worked example (subject-verb agreement).** "The box of markers *are* on the desk." → The subject is
> *box* (singular), so: "The box of markers **is** on the desk." (The prepositional phrase *of markers*
> does not change the subject.)

> **Watch Out:** *Its* = belonging to it; *it's* = it is. *Their* = belonging to them; *there* = a
> place; *they're* = they are. These are tested often.

> **Try It.** Fix: "Me and him walked to there house." → *(He and I walked to their house.)*

---

### Revising & Editing Checklist
- ☐ 10B Structure: intro, transitions, coherent paragraphs, conclusion; developed ideas
- ☐ 10C Revise for clarity, organization, style, word choice, sentence variety
- ☐ 10D·i–ii Complete sentences, subject-verb agreement, consistent verb tense/voice
- ☐ 10D·iii–vi Prepositions, pronoun-antecedent, capitalization, punctuation
- ☐ 10D·vii Spelling and commonly confused words
