# Short Constructed Response (SCR) Mastery

The Reading section includes **2 Short Constructed Responses**, each worth **2 points.** You write a
few sentences that answer a question and back it up with **evidence from the text.** *(Builds on
response standards 6C and 6D.)*

---

## What the Scorer Looks For

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| **2** | A **correct, clear answer** that is fully supported with **accurate text evidence** and a brief explanation |
| **1** | A partially correct answer, or an answer with weak, vague, or incomplete evidence |
| **0** | An incorrect answer, no text evidence, or a blank/off-topic response |

> **Key Idea:** Two points = **answer + evidence + explanation.** Leaving out the evidence is the most
> common way students lose a point.

---

## The A-E-E Structure

1. **Answer** the question directly in one sentence.
2. **Evidence** — quote or paraphrase a specific detail from the text.
3. **Explain** how that evidence supports your answer.

> **Sentence stems:**
> - *The text shows that…*
> - *For example, the author writes, "…"*
> - *This proves/suggests that…*

---

## Worked Example

**Prompt:** *How does the narrator feel about moving to a new town? Use evidence from the text.*

**Sample — Full credit (2):**
> The narrator feels **nervous and sad** about moving. The text says she "stared at the empty walls of
> her old room and felt her stomach tighten." This shows she is anxious because her familiar home is
> gone.

*Why it scores 2:* clear answer (nervous/sad), specific text evidence (the quotation), and an
explanation tying them together.

**Sample — Partial credit (1):**
> The narrator feels bad about moving because she doesn't want to go.

*Why it scores 1:* the answer is reasonable but there is **no text evidence** and no explanation.

**Sample — No credit (0):**
> I would feel excited to move to a new place.

*Why it scores 0:* it gives a personal opinion, not the **narrator's** feelings, and uses no evidence.

---

> **Strategy:** Keep it short and focused — three or four sentences is plenty. Always include at least
> one specific detail or short quotation from the passage. Answer the exact question asked; do not
> drift into your own opinion unless the prompt asks for it.
