# Inside the STAAR Grade 8 Test

## The Final STAAR Year

STAAR — the State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness — measures how well students have
learned the **Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS)** for their grade. Under Texas House Bill 8
(2025), **spring 2027 is the last STAAR administration** for grades 3–8. A new assessment called the
Student Success Tool is scheduled to replace it in 2027–2028. This book prepares you fully for that
final STAAR year.

## A Computer-Based Test

STAAR is given **online**, on a computer. That means the real test includes question types that go
beyond simple multiple choice — you might type an answer, choose from a drop-down menu, drag items
into place, or build an equation. You cannot practice clicking and dragging in a paper book, so this
book does the next best thing: it **shows every online question type on paper** and teaches you
exactly how to answer it. The *thinking* is identical; only the clicking is different. The **Item-Type
Field Guide** (next section) walks through each one.

## What Grade 8 Students Take

At Grade 8, students are assessed in **Reading Language Arts, Mathematics, Science, and Social
Studies.** This book focuses on the two tested every year and taken by every Grade 8 student:
**Reading Language Arts and Mathematics.**

## The Mathematics Test at a Glance

| Feature | Grade 8 Mathematics |
|---|---|
| Total questions | **40** |
| Total points | **48** (32 one-point questions + 8 two-point questions) |
| Reporting categories (strands) | 4 — Numerical; Computations & Algebraic; Geometry & Measurement; Data & Financial Literacy |
| Question types | Multiple choice, multiselect, inline choice (drop-down), text entry/griddable, drag-and-drop, equation editor, graphing |
| Tools provided | **Calculator** and the **Grade 8 Mathematics Reference Sheet** (formulas) |

## The Reading Language Arts Test at a Glance

| Feature | Grade 8 Reading Language Arts |
|---|---|
| Total questions | **~45** |
| Total points | **56** |
| Reading section | ~28 questions / ~30 points — includes **2 Short Constructed Responses** (2 points each) |
| Writing section (Revising & Editing) | ~17 questions / ~26 points — includes **1 Extended Constructed Response** (10 points) |
| Passages | Literary and informational; single passages and a paired set |
| Question types | Multiple choice, multiselect, inline choice, text entry, hot-text, plus written responses |
| Tools provided | Online **dictionary/thesaurus** |

## How Much Time?

STAAR for grades 3–8 is given with a generous time window of **up to four hours**. It is **not a speed
test** — most students finish comfortably within that window. Still, practicing with a timer builds
the steady pace that keeps you calm on test day.

> **Watch Out:** Because writing responses (the SCR and ECR) are worth real points — up to **14 of the
> 56 RLA points** come from written responses — never leave them blank. A partial, organized response
> almost always earns more than nothing.

# How STAAR Is Scored

## From Raw Points to a Performance Level

Each test question earns **raw points** (1 or 2 for most questions; the Extended Constructed Response
is worth 10). Your total raw points are converted to a **scale score**, and that scale score places
you in one of four **performance levels**:

| Performance Level | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Masters Grade Level** | Strong command of the grade's content; ready for the next grade with little support. |
| **Meets Grade Level** | Solid understanding; likely to succeed next grade with some support. |
| **Approaches Grade Level** | Some understanding; likely needs targeted support next grade. |
| **Did Not Meet Grade Level** | Not enough understanding yet; needs significant support. |

The main goal for most students is to reach **Meets Grade Level** or higher — and this book is built
to get you there.

## How Written Responses Are Scored

- **Short Constructed Response (SCR)** — a few sentences answering a specific question, scored **0 to
  2 points** using a rubric. You earn full credit by making a clear point and backing it with correct
  **evidence from the text**.
- **Extended Constructed Response (ECR)** — a full multi-paragraph essay, scored **0 to 10 points**.
  The rubric rewards a clear central idea or claim, organized paragraphs, specific supporting
  evidence, and correct use of language and conventions.

You will learn to earn these points step by step in the **SCR Mastery** and **ECR Mastery** sections,
which include real-style prompts and sample responses scored at high, middle, and low levels so you
can see exactly what earns points.

## Tracking Your Own Scores

After each practice test, use the **Score-Tracking Sheets** and **Strand Diagnostic Charts** at the
back of this book. They turn your raw points into a performance estimate and, more importantly, show
you **which strand or skill to study next.** Watching your scores climb from Test 1 to Test 4 is the
clearest sign your hard work is paying off.
