# Item-Type Field Guide
### How Online Questions Look on Paper

STAAR is taken on a computer, so some questions ask you to do more than bubble a letter. This guide
shows **every question type you may meet**, how we present it on paper in this book, and a quick
example. The skill being tested is always a Grade 8 TEKS skill — only the way you record your answer
changes.

> **Key Idea:** On screen you *click, type, or drag.* On paper you *circle, write, or draw an arrow.*
> The thinking is exactly the same.

---

## 1. Multiple Choice
**Online:** Click one answer choice (A–D). **On paper:** Circle the letter of the one best answer.

> *Example (Math).* Which number is the greatest?
> A. 0.6 B. ⅗ C. 0.58 D. ½
> *(Answer: A)*

## 2. Multiselect
**Online:** Check every correct box. **On paper:** Circle **all** correct letters. The question tells
you how many to pick.

> *Example (Math).* Select the **two** numbers that are irrational.
> A. √49 B. √10 C. π D. 0.25 *(Answers: B and C)*

## 3. Inline Choice (Drop-Down)
**Online:** Click a drop-down menu inside the sentence and pick the correct option. **On paper:** Each
drop-down is shown as a box with choices; circle the correct option in each box.

> *Example (Revising & Editing).* The science club meet after school, and the members \[ **is / are / was** \] planning a fundraiser. *(Answer: are)*

## 4. Text Entry / Griddable
**Online:** Type a number or short answer into a box; for numbers, you may fill a grid. **On paper:**
Write your answer in the box, then bubble the answer grid when one is provided.

> *Example (Math).* A line passes through (0, 3) with a slope of 2. What is the value of *y* when
> *x* = 4? Write your answer: ______ *(Answer: 11)*

## 5. Drag-and-Drop
**Online:** Drag words or numbers from a bank into blanks or an order. **On paper:** A word/number
bank is printed; write each item on the correct line or draw an arrow to its spot.

> *Example (Math).* Order these from **least to greatest** by writing them on the lines.
> Bank: 0.4, ⅓, 0.37, ⅖
> Least ___ ___ ___ ___ Greatest *(Answer: ⅓, 0.37, 0.4, ⅖)*

## 6. Equation Editor
**Online:** Build an expression or equation using an on-screen math keypad. **On paper:** Write the
expression or equation neatly on the line.

> *Example (Math).* Write an equation in the form *y = mx + b* for a line with slope 3 and
> *y*-intercept −5. ________________ *(Answer: y = 3x − 5)*

## 7. Graphing
**Online:** Plot points, lines, or bars on a grid. **On paper:** Use the printed coordinate grid and a
pencil to plot the points or draw the line.

> *Example (Math).* On the grid provided, plot the point (−2, 4) and draw the line *y = x + 1.*

## 8. Hot Text
**Online:** Click a sentence or phrase in the passage to select it. **On paper:** Underline or write
the number of the sentence that answers the question.

> *Example (Reading).* Underline the sentence from the passage that **best** supports the idea that
> the narrator felt nervous.

## 9. Written Responses (SCR and ECR)
**Online:** Type your response in a text box. **On paper:** Write in the lined space provided.

- **Short Constructed Response (SCR)** — a few sentences with text evidence (2 points).
- **Extended Constructed Response (ECR)** — a full multi-paragraph essay (10 points).

> You will practice these in depth in the **SCR Mastery** and **ECR Mastery** sections.

---

> **Strategy:** When a question says "Select TWO" or "least to greatest," underline those words before
> you answer. Many points are lost not from wrong math or reading, but from missing the directions.
