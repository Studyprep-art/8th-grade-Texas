# Glossary of Literary & Craft Terms

These are the terms the STAAR Grade 8 Reading standards expect you to know. Learn them well—questions
often use these exact words.

## Story Elements
- **Theme** — the message or lesson of a text, shown through characters and events. *(7A)*
- **Character motivation** — the reason a character acts as they do. *(7B)*
- **Flashback** — a scene that jumps back to an earlier time. *(7C)*
- **Foreshadowing** — an early hint about what will happen later. *(7C)*
- **Subplot** — a smaller story that runs alongside the main plot. *(7C)*
- **Setting** — when and where a story takes place. *(7D)*

## Figurative Language & Devices
- **Simile** — a comparison using *like* or *as* ("quiet as a mouse"). *(9D)*
- **Metaphor** — a comparison without *like/as* ("time is a thief"). *(9D)*
- **Extended metaphor** — a metaphor developed over several lines. *(9D)*
- **Personification** — giving human traits to something non-human. *(9D)*
- **Irony** — a contrast between what is expected and what happens. *(9E)*
- **Point of view** — who is telling the story (first person, third person). *(9E)*
- **Mood** — the feeling a text creates in the reader. *(9F)*
- **Tone** — the author's attitude toward the subject. *(9F)*
- **Voice** — the author's distinct personality in the writing. *(9F)*

## Rhetoric & Argument
- **Analogy** — a comparison that explains one thing using another. *(9G)*
- **Juxtaposition** — placing two things side by side for contrast. *(9G)*
- **Bandwagon appeal** — "everyone is doing it, so you should too." *(9G)*
- **Circular reasoning** — using a claim to prove itself. *(9G)*
- **Claim** — the main position an argument defends. *(8E)*
- **Counter-argument** — the opposing side, which a strong writer answers. *(8E)*
- **Audience** — the readers a text is written for. *(8E)*
- **Thesis / controlling idea** — the main point of an essay or informational text. *(8D, 10B)*

## Genres
- **Fiction / literary nonfiction** — made-up stories / true stories told with craft.
- **Poetry** — written in lines and stanzas; watch line length and punctuation. *(8B)*
- **Drama** — a play in **acts and scenes** with dialogue and stage directions. *(8C)*
- **Informational / argumentative / persuasive** — explains a topic / defends a claim / convinces.
- **Correspondence** — letters and emails (friendly or business). *(11D)*
