# Strand 2 · Computations and Algebraic Relationships

This is the **largest strand** on the test (about 15 of 40 questions). It is all about **lines,
relationships, and equations.** Master it and you master most of the Math test.

**Standards covered in this strand:**
- **○ 8.4A** — Use similar right triangles to understand that slope is the same between any two points on a line.
- **★ 8.4B** — Graph proportional relationships; interpret the unit rate as the slope.
- **★ 8.4C** — Use a table or graph to find the rate of change (slope) and the y-intercept.
- **○ 8.5A** — Represent linear proportional situations as y = kx.
- **○ 8.5B** — Represent linear non-proportional situations as y = mx + b (b ≠ 0).
- **○ 8.5E** — Solve problems involving direct variation.
- **○ 8.5F** — Distinguish proportional from non-proportional situations.
- **★ 8.5G** — Identify functions from ordered pairs, tables, mappings, and graphs.
- **○ 8.5H** — Identify examples of proportional and non-proportional functions.
- **★ 8.5I** — Write an equation y = mx + b to model a linear relationship.
- **○ 8.8A** — Write one-variable equations or inequalities with variables on both sides.
- **○ 8.8B** — Write a real-world problem for a given one-variable equation.
- **★ 8.8C** — Model and solve one-variable equations with variables on both sides.
- **○ 8.9A** — Identify the solution of two linear equations from the intersection of their graphs.

## Part A · Slope and Rate of Change  (8.4A ○, 8.4B ★, 8.4C ★)

**Slope** measures how steep a line is: how much *y* changes for each change in *x*.

$$\text{slope} = m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

- **8.4A — Why slope is constant.** Pick any two points on a line and draw a right triangle down and
  across to them. Every such triangle on the same line is **similar**, so the ratio (rise over run) is
  the **same everywhere** — that ratio is the slope.
- **8.4B — Proportional graphs.** A **proportional** relationship passes through the **origin (0, 0)**.
  Its equation is **y = kx**, and the **unit rate** (cost per item, miles per hour) **is the slope k.**
- **8.4C — From a table or graph.** Slope = (change in y) ÷ (change in x). The **y-intercept** is the
  y-value when **x = 0.**

**Worked example (8.4C).** A table shows (0, 2), (1, 5), (2, 8).
- Slope = (5 − 2) ÷ (1 − 0) = **3.** y-intercept = value at x = 0 = **2.** Equation: y = 3x + 2.

> **Watch Out:** Rise over run, not run over rise. Count the **vertical** change first, then the
> horizontal.

> **Try It.** A line goes through (0, 0) and (4, 10). What is the slope? *(Answer: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5.)*

## Part B · Proportional vs. Non-Proportional  (8.5A ○, 8.5B ○, 8.5E ○, 8.5F ○, 8.5H ○)

| | Proportional | Non-Proportional |
|---|---|---|
| Equation | **y = kx** | **y = mx + b** (b ≠ 0) |
| Graph | Straight line **through the origin** | Straight line **not** through the origin |
| Table | Constant ratio y⁄x = k | y⁄x is **not** constant |

- **8.5E — Direct variation** is just a proportional relationship: **y = kx,** where **k** is the
  constant of proportionality. If y = 15 when x = 3, then k = 15 ÷ 3 = 5, so y = 5x.
- **8.5F / 8.5H — Telling them apart.** Ask: *Does the line pass through (0, 0)?* If yes →
  proportional. If it has a starting amount (a y-intercept that is not 0) → non-proportional.

**Worked example (8.5B).** A gym charges a $20 sign-up fee plus $5 per visit. Cost y for x visits:
**y = 5x + 20.** Because of the $20 fee (b = 20 ≠ 0), this is **non-proportional.**

> **Watch Out:** A "per item" rate with **no** starting fee is proportional. Add any flat fee and it
> becomes non-proportional.

> **Try It.** Is y = 7x proportional or non-proportional? *(Answer: Proportional — it passes through the origin, b = 0.)*

## Part C · Functions  (8.5G ★)

A **function** pairs **each input (x) with exactly one output (y).**
- **Ordered pairs / tables / mappings:** an x-value may **not** repeat with two different y-values.
- **Graphs:** use the **vertical line test** — if any vertical line crosses the graph more than once,
  it is **not** a function.

**Worked example.** Is {(1, 3), (2, 5), (1, 7)} a function? **No** — the input 1 is paired with both
3 and 7.

> **Try It.** Does a straight (non-vertical) line pass the vertical line test? *(Answer: Yes — it is a function.)*

## Part D · Writing Linear Equations  (8.5I ★)

To write **y = mx + b**, find two things: the **slope m** and the **y-intercept b.**

**Worked example.** A plant is 4 cm tall and grows 2 cm each week. Model its height y after x weeks.
- Growth rate (slope) m = **2.** Starting height (y-intercept) b = **4.**
- Equation: **y = 2x + 4.**

> **Strategy:** "Starting amount" is almost always **b**; "rate per unit" is almost always **m.**

> **Try It.** A taxi charges $3 to start plus $2 per mile. Write the equation for cost y after x miles.
> *(Answer: y = 2x + 3.)*

## Part E · One-Variable Equations and Inequalities  (8.8A ○, 8.8B ○, 8.8C ★)

When the variable appears on **both sides,** collect the variable terms on one side and the numbers on
the other.

**Worked example (8.8C).** Solve 3x + 5 = x − 7.
- Subtract x: 2x + 5 = −7
- Subtract 5: 2x = −12
- Divide by 2: **x = −6**

- **8.8A** — Write such equations/inequalities from a situation.
- **8.8B** — Do the reverse: given 3x + 5 = x − 7, describe a real situation it could model.

> **Watch Out:** Whatever you do to one side, do to the **other** side. Keep the equation balanced.

> **Try It.** Solve 4x − 3 = 2x + 9. *(Answer: 2x = 12, x = 6.)*

---

## Part F · Systems of Two Linear Equations  (8.9A ○)

Two lines graphed together **intersect** at the point that **satisfies both equations.** That
intersection point **(x, y)** is the **solution** of the system.

**Worked example.** Lines y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5 cross at **(2, 3).** Check: 3 = 2 + 1 ✓ and
3 = −2 + 5 ✓. The solution is (2, 3).

> **Try It.** If two lines cross at (−1, 4), what is the solution of the system? *(Answer: x = −1, y = 4.)*
