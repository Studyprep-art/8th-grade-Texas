# Strand 1 · Numerical Representations and Relationships

**What this strand tests:** how to represent, compare, and order real numbers — including irrational
numbers and scientific notation.

**Standards covered in this strand:**
- **○ 8.2A** — Describe relationships between sets of real numbers using a visual representation.
- **○ 8.2B** — Approximate the value of an irrational number (including π and square roots of numbers less than 225) and locate it on a number line.
- **○ 8.2C** — Convert between standard decimal notation and scientific notation.
- **★ 8.2D** — Order a set of real numbers from mathematical and real-world contexts. *(Readiness)*

---

## 8.2A · The Real Number System  ○

Every number you use at Grade 8 is a **real number.** Real numbers divide into groups that fit inside
one another:

- **Natural numbers:** 1, 2, 3, … (counting numbers)
- **Whole numbers:** 0, 1, 2, 3, … (naturals plus 0)
- **Integers:** … −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, … (whole numbers plus their opposites)
- **Rational numbers:** any number that can be written as a fraction a⁄b — this includes integers,
  terminating decimals (0.75), and repeating decimals (0.333…).
- **Irrational numbers:** numbers that **cannot** be written as a simple fraction and whose decimals
  never end or repeat — such as **π** and **√2**.

> **Key Idea:** The groups are *nested* — every natural number is also whole, an integer, and rational.
> But rational and irrational never overlap: a number is one or the other, never both.

**Worked example.** Classify √16 and √20.
- √16 = 4, which is a natural number, whole, integer, and **rational.**
- √20 ≈ 4.472… never ends or repeats, so it is **irrational.**

> **Watch Out:** A square root is only irrational when the number is **not** a perfect square. √25 = 5
> is rational; √26 is irrational.

> **Try It.** True or false: every integer is a rational number. *(Answer: True — any integer n equals n⁄1.)*

---

## 8.2B · Approximating Irrational Numbers  ○

To estimate a square root, find the two **perfect squares** it falls between.

**Worked example.** Approximate √50 and place it on a number line.
- 49 = 7² and 64 = 8², so √50 is between **7 and 8.**
- 50 is just above 49, so √50 ≈ **7.1.**
- On a number line, mark it just to the right of 7.

**π** is a known irrational number: **π ≈ 3.14,** so it sits between 3 and 4, just past 3.1.

> **Watch Out:** "Less than 225" in standard 8.2B means the number under the root is below 225 — so
> you work with roots up to about √224 ≈ 14.97.

> **Try It.** Between which two whole numbers is √30? *(Answer: between 5 and 6, since 25 < 30 < 36.)*

---

## 8.2C · Scientific Notation  ○

Scientific notation writes a number as **a × 10ⁿ**, where **1 ≤ |a| < 10** and n is an integer.
- **Large numbers** → positive exponent. **Small numbers (less than 1)** → negative exponent.

**Worked examples.**
- 4,500,000 = **4.5 × 10⁶** (move the decimal 6 places left).
- 0.00032 = **3.2 × 10⁻⁴** (move the decimal 4 places right).
- 7.1 × 10⁵ = **710,000** (move the decimal 5 places right).

> **Watch Out:** The factor *a* must be at least 1 and less than 10. Writing 45 × 10⁵ is **not** correct
> scientific notation — fix it to 4.5 × 10⁶.

> **Try It.** Write 0.0067 in scientific notation. *(Answer: 6.7 × 10⁻³.)*

---

## 8.2D · Ordering Real Numbers  ★ *(Readiness)*

Numbers on the test often appear in different forms at once — fractions, decimals, percents, square
roots, and scientific notation. **Convert them all to decimals, then compare.**

**Worked example.** Order from least to greatest: ⅗, 0.58, √(0.36), 62%.
- ⅗ = 0.60
- 0.58 = 0.58
- √0.36 = 0.6 = 0.60
- 62% = 0.62
- Compare: 0.58 < 0.60 = 0.60 < 0.62
- **Order:** 0.58, then ⅗ and √0.36 (equal), then 62%.

> **Key Idea:** One common form — usually the decimal — lets you compare very different-looking numbers
> fairly. A number line helps you *see* the order.

> **Watch Out:** On a negative number line, the number farther **left** is **smaller.** −0.8 is less
> than −0.3.

> **Try It.** Order from greatest to least: 0.7, ⅔, 71%. *(Answer: 71% (0.71), 0.7, ⅔ (≈0.667).)*
