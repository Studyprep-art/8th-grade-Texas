# Strand 4 · Data Analysis and Personal Financial Literacy

This strand (about 8 of 40 questions) has two halves: **reading data** (scatterplots, trend lines,
spread) and **personal finance** (interest, saving, the cost of borrowing).

**Standards covered in this strand:**

- **★ 8.5D** — Use a trend line that approximates a linear relationship to make predictions.
- **○ 8.11A** — Construct a scatterplot and describe the association (linear, non-linear, or none).
- **○ 8.11B** — Determine the mean absolute deviation (data set of at most 10 points).
- **○ 8.12A** — Compare how interest rate and loan length affect the cost of credit.
- **○ 8.12C** — Explain how small amounts invested regularly grow over time.

## Part A · Scatterplots and Association  (8.5C ○, 8.11A ○)

A **scatterplot** graphs paired (bivariate) data as points. The pattern shows the **association**:
- **Positive linear** — points rise left to right.
- **Negative linear** — points fall left to right.
- **Non-linear** — points follow a curve.
- **No association** — points are scattered with no pattern.

> **Worked example.** As study time goes up and test scores go up, the points rise to the right — a
> **positive linear** association.

> **Try It.** Hours of TV watched vs. test grade, where grades fall as TV rises — what association?
> *(Answer: negative linear.)*

---

## Part B · Trend Lines and Predictions  (8.5D ★)

A **trend line** (line of best fit) is a straight line drawn through the middle of the data. Use its
pattern to **predict** values you did not measure.

> **Worked example.** A trend line for plant height follows y = 2x + 3 (x = weeks). Predict the height
> at week 10: y = 2(10) + 3 = **23 cm.**

> **Watch Out:** A prediction is an **estimate**, not an exact value — real points scatter around the
> trend line.

---

## Part C · Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)  (8.11B ○)

MAD measures how spread out data are — the **average distance of each value from the mean.**
**Steps:** (1) find the mean, (2) find each value's distance from the mean, (3) average those distances.

> **Worked example.** Data: 2, 4, 6, 8. Mean = 5. Distances: 3, 1, 1, 3. MAD = (3+1+1+3) ÷ 4 = **2.**

> **Try It.** Find the MAD of 10, 10, 20, 20. *(Mean 15; distances 5,5,5,5; MAD = 5.)*

## Part D · Personal Financial Literacy  (8.12A ○, 8.12C ○, 8.12D ★, 8.12G ○)

**Simple interest:** **I = Prt** (Principal × rate × time in years).
**Compound interest:** **A = P(1 + r⁄n)^(nt)** — interest earns interest, so it grows faster.

> **Worked example (8.12D).** $500 at 4% for 3 years, **simple** interest: I = 500 × 0.04 × 3 = **$60.**
> With **compound** interest, you would earn slightly more because each year's interest also earns
> interest.

- **8.12A — Cost of credit.** A **higher interest rate** or a **longer loan** means you pay **more**
  interest overall. Borrowing is not free.
- **8.12C — Saving regularly.** Small amounts saved consistently add up and grow, especially with
  compound interest and time.
- **8.12G — College savings.** Estimate total cost (tuition, fees, housing), subtract expected family
  contribution, then divide the rest across the months you have to save.

> **Try It.** Find the simple interest on $1,000 at 5% for 2 years. *(Answer: I = 1000 × 0.05 × 2 = $100.)*
