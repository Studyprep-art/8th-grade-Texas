## Part A · Dilations and Similarity  (8.3A ○, 8.3B ○, 8.3C ★, 8.10D ○)

A **dilation** resizes a figure by a **scale factor k** from a center point. With the **origin** as
center, the rule is:

**(x, y) → (kx, ky)**

- **8.3A / 8.3B — Similar shapes.** A figure and its dilation are **similar**: corresponding sides are
  **proportional** (all multiplied by k) and angles stay the **same.**
- **8.10D — Effect on measurements.** Lengths multiply by **k**; **area** multiplies by **k².**

**Worked example (8.3C).** Triangle with vertex (2, 3) is dilated by scale factor 3 about the origin.
New vertex: (3·2, 3·3) = **(6, 9).**

> **Watch Out:** k > 1 makes the figure **larger**; 0 < k < 1 makes it **smaller.** Area grows or
> shrinks by the **square** of k — a scale factor of 2 makes the area **4 times** as large.

> **Try It.** A square of side 5 is dilated by k = 2. What is the new side length? *(Answer: 10.)*

## Part B · Transformations on the Coordinate Plane  (8.10A ○, 8.10B ○, 8.10C ★)

| Transformation | Algebraic rule | Changes size? |
|---|---|---|
| Translation (slide) | (x, y) → (x + a, y + b) | No — congruent |
| Reflection over x-axis | (x, y) → (x, −y) | No — congruent |
| Reflection over y-axis | (x, y) → (−x, y) | No — congruent |
| Rotation 180° about origin | (x, y) → (−x, −y) | No — congruent |
| Rotation 90° counterclockwise | (x, y) → (−y, x) | No — congruent |
| Dilation (factor k) | (x, y) → (kx, ky) | **Yes** — similar, not congruent |

- **8.10A / 8.10B — Congruence.** Translations, reflections, and rotations are **rigid**: they keep the
  same size and shape, so the image is **congruent** to the original. A **dilation** (k ≠ 1) changes
  size, so it is **not** congruent — only **similar.**

**Worked example (8.10C).** Reflect point (4, −2) over the x-axis. Rule (x, −y): → **(4, 2).**

> **Watch Out:** Reflecting over the **x-axis** flips the **y**-sign; over the **y-axis** flips the
> **x**-sign.

> **Try It.** Rotate (3, 5) by 180° about the origin. *(Answer: (−3, −5).)*

## Part C · Angle Relationships  (8.8D ○)

- **Triangle angle sum:** the three interior angles add to **180°.**
- **Exterior angle:** equals the **sum of the two remote (non-adjacent) interior angles.**
- **Parallel lines cut by a transversal:** corresponding angles are equal; alternate interior angles
  are equal.
- **Angle-Angle (AA) similarity:** if two triangles share two equal angles, they are **similar.**

**Worked example.** A triangle has angles 50° and 60°. The third angle = 180 − 50 − 60 = **70°.**

> **Try It.** Two angles of a triangle are 90° and 35°. Find the third. *(Answer: 55°.)*

## Part D · Volume  (8.6A ○, 8.7A ★)

Use **B** for the area of the base.

- **Cylinder:** V = Bh = **πr²h** (8.6A explains why: stack of circular bases).
- **Cone:** V = **⅓Bh = ⅓πr²h** (one-third of a cylinder with the same base and height).
- **Sphere:** V = **4⁄3 πr³.**

**Worked example (8.7A).** A cylinder has radius 3 and height 10. V = πr²h = π(3²)(10) = **90π ≈ 282.6
cubic units.**

> **Watch Out:** The cone is **⅓** of the matching cylinder — don't forget the one-third.

> **Try It.** Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 and height 10. *(Answer: ⅓·90π = 30π ≈ 94.2.)*

## Part E · Surface Area  (8.7B ★)

Surface area is the **total area of all the outside surfaces.**

- **Prism — total:** S = Ph + 2B (P = base perimeter, B = base area).
- **Cylinder — total:** S = 2πrh + 2πr² (the side plus the two circles).

**Worked example (8.7B).** A cylinder has radius 2 and height 5.
S = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2π(2)(5) + 2π(2²) = 20π + 8π = **28π ≈ 87.9 square units.**

> **Strategy:** "Lateral" area is just the **side**; "total" adds the **bases.** Read which one the
> question asks for.

> **Try It.** Find the lateral surface area of that cylinder. *(Answer: 2πrh = 20π ≈ 62.8.)*

## Part F · The Pythagorean Theorem  (8.6C ○, 8.7C ★, 8.7D ○)

For a **right triangle** with legs a and b and hypotenuse c:

**a² + b² = c²**

- **8.7C — Solve and the converse.** Find a missing side; or, to test whether a triangle is **right,**
  check if a² + b² = c².
- **8.7D — Distance between two points.** The horizontal and vertical gaps are the legs; the straight
  distance is the hypotenuse.

**Worked example (8.7C).** Legs 6 and 8: c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, so c = **10.**

**Worked example (8.7D).** Distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6): legs 3 and 4 → √(9 + 16) = **5.**

> **Watch Out:** The hypotenuse **c** is always across from the right angle and is the **longest** side.

> **Try It.** Is a triangle with sides 5, 12, 13 a right triangle? *(Answer: Yes — 25 + 144 = 169 = 13².)*
