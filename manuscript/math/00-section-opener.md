# Part 1 · Mathematics
## How Grade 8 Math Is Built

The Grade 8 Mathematics test has **40 questions worth 48 points.** Every question is drawn from one of
**four strands** (the state calls them reporting categories). Knowing the strands tells you exactly
what to study — and this book teaches **every skill in every strand.**

| Strand | What It Covers | On the Test |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Numerical Representations & Relationships** | Real numbers, irrational numbers, scientific notation, ordering numbers | ~4 questions |
| **2 · Computations & Algebraic Relationships** | Slope, linear equations, functions, one-variable equations | ~15 questions (the largest) |
| **3 · Geometry & Measurement** | Dilations, transformations, volume, surface area, Pythagorean theorem, angles | ~13 questions |
| **4 · Data Analysis & Personal Financial Literacy** | Scatterplots, trend lines, mean absolute deviation, interest, saving | ~8 questions |

### Readiness vs. Supporting Standards
Every skill is labeled one of two ways:
- **★ Readiness standard** — tested heavily every year. These carry most of the points, so master them first.
- **○ Supporting standard** — tested less often, but still fair game.

### The Process Standards (8.1 A–G)
Beyond the four strands, STAAR weaves in **mathematical process standards**: applying math to real
situations, choosing a problem-solving strategy, using tools, and explaining your reasoning. You will
practice these inside every lesson and test — they are *how* you do the math, not a separate topic.

> **Key Idea:** Read each lesson, do the warm-up drills, then prove it on the practice tests. Use the
> **reference sheet** on the next page the whole way through — it is yours on the real test, too.
