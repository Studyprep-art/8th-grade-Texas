# A Guide for Parents and Teachers

This book is built so a student can use it alone, but your support makes it far more powerful. Here is
how to help.

## Start With the Diagnostics
The **Score-Tracking Sheet** and **Strand Diagnostic Chart** turn each test into a study plan. After a
test, help the student find the weakest strand or skill area and go back to that lesson first. Progress,
not perfection, is the goal—watch for scores rising across Tests 1–4.

## Coaching the Written Responses
The SCR and ECR are where many students leave points on the table. You do **not** need to grade like
the state; instead, ask three questions:
1. **Did you answer the exact question?**
2. **Did you use specific evidence from the text?**
3. **Did you explain how the evidence supports your point?**

Compare the student's writing to the **scored samples** in this book. Seeing the difference between a
high and a middle response teaches more than a grade ever could.

## Keep It Positive and Steady
- Short, regular practice beats long, rare cram sessions. Use the 4-week or 8-week calendar.
- Praise **effort and strategy** ("You used two pieces of evidence!"), not just the score.
- Normalize mistakes—every wrong answer with a good explanation is a lesson learned.

## On Test Day
Remind the student of the **Test-Day Game Plan**: read directions carefully, answer easy questions
first, never leave a blank, and give the written responses real effort. A calm, prepared student does
their best work.

> **A note on the 2027 test:** Spring 2027 is the final STAAR administration for grades 3–8. Whatever
> assessment follows, the skills in this book—reading closely, reasoning with evidence, solving
> real-world math—will continue to serve your student well.
